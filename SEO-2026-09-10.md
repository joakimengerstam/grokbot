# grokbot.se SEO pass, 2026-09-10

19 files modified, 3 new, `npm run build` green, 13 pages built. Deployed the
same day as commit `ed148a1`, minus three files. See addendum 3.

Baseline: the live site was already in decent shape. Every page had a unique
title and description, a canonical, an hreflang pair, exactly one `h1`, and
between 373 and 1517 words. No images, so no missing alt text. `www` and `http`
both redirect correctly. Search Console is verified, on the `joakimulbert@gmail.com`
account. Real numbers are in addendum 2.

Four real problems, one of which touched every URL on the site.

## 1. Every canonical and every internal link pointed at a redirect

GitHub Pages serves this site from directories, so `/setup` 301s to `/setup/`.
The build was emitting the other form everywhere.

The cause was one line in `Layout.astro`:

```js
const abs = (p) => new URL(...).href.replace(/\/$/, '') || ...
```

That `.replace()` stripped the trailing slash off every absolute URL the layout
produced. Meanwhile `@astrojs/sitemap` emits them with the slash. Result:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| canonical | `https://grokbot.se/setup` (301s) | `https://grokbot.se/setup/` |
| hreflang | `.../setup`, `.../sv/installning` (301s) | both with slash |
| sitemap | `.../setup/` | `.../setup/` |
| nav and in-body links | `/setup` (301s) | `/setup/` |

So 12 canonicals, 36 hreflang entries and every internal link on the site sent
crawlers and visitors through a redirect hop, and the sitemap disagreed with all
of them about which URL was real.

Fixed in four places: `trailingSlash: 'always'` in `astro.config.mjs`, a single
`withSlash()` helper exported from `src/lib/i18n.ts`, that helper applied to
`abs()` and to every nav, footer and language-switcher href in `Layout.astro`,
and trailing slashes added to the 16 in-body links across the page files.

Verified after build: canonical, hreflang and sitemap agree on all 12 URLs, and
a crawl of `dist/` finds zero internal links missing a slash.

## 2. The share card was a 272-byte SVG favicon

```
og:image      https://grokbot.se/favicon.svg    (image/svg+xml, 272 bytes)
twitter:card  summary
```

Facebook, LinkedIn, Slack and X do not render SVG as an OG image. Combined with
`summary` rather than `summary_large_image`, every share of this site produced a
blank or fallback card. For a site whose distribution is people posting links,
that is a direct cost.

Two real 1200x630 PNGs now live in `public/`, built from the site's own palette
and type (IBM Plex Sans, cobalt `#1e3fe0`, paper `#f4f6f8`), one per language:

- `og.png` carries the English `h1`, "Give your AI a job description."
- `og-sv.png` carries the Swedish one, "Ge din AI en arbetsbeskrivning."

`Layout.astro` picks by `lang`. Added `og:image:type`, `:width`, `:height`,
`:alt`, `twitter:image`, and switched the card to `summary_large_image`.

## 3. No article schema and nothing machine-readable about freshness

Every page carried the same two nodes, `WebSite` and `Organization`, and nothing
else. For a guide site that competes on being current, and whose titles literally
say "updated September 2026", there was no `dateModified` anywhere a machine
could read it.

`Layout.astro` now takes three optional props and emits accordingly:

- `datePublished` / `dateModified` produce a `TechArticle` node with headline,
  description, `inLanguage`, both dates, `mainEntityOfPage`, image, author
  (Joakim Engerstam) and publisher (the Roore `@id`). Also emits
  `article:published_time` and `article:modified_time`.
- `breadcrumb` produces a two-level `BreadcrumbList`, home then current page,
  with labels matching the nav.

Applied to all 12 pages. The two language homes get `TechArticle` but no
breadcrumb, since they are the root of their own trail. `datePublished` values
come from each file's first commit; `dateModified` is today for all 12, which is
accurate because all 12 changed in this pass.

The sitemap now carries `lastmod` too, set from a constant in `astro.config.mjs`
rather than the build clock, so a rebuild with no content change does not claim
the content changed.

I did not add sitemap-level `xhtml:link` alternates. `@astrojs/sitemap`'s `i18n`
option detects locale from a path prefix, and English here has no prefix, so it
would emit wrong alternates for six of the twelve URLs. The `<link rel="alternate">`
tags in the head are a complete signal on their own.

## 5. Em dashes, English only

24 em dashes in source, all on the English side. The Swedish pages had zero, so
the Swedish was de-slopped at some point and the English translation
reintroduced them. On a site about AI, prose that reads as machine-written is a
specific problem rather than a general one.

All 24 removed: comma where the clause continued, period where it was really two
sentences, parentheses for the one pair that bracketed an aside. The Swedish
en dashes (`–`) were left alone, since that is correct Swedish typography and a
different character.

Two were in code comments (`global.css`, `roore.ts`) and are also gone.

While in `index.astro` I also cut a conditional hedge that had crossed over from
an earlier version of the Roore copy: "If it works, we take the next flow. If
not, it stops there." That frames the doubt as "the product might not work"
rather than "you decide what happens next", which is the opposite of what the
risk reversal is for. It now reads "we build that first, at a fixed price. Then
you decide which flow is next." The same line was cut from roore.se for the same
reason.

## 6. New 404

`src/pages/404.astro`, which Astro builds to `dist/404.html` and GitHub Pages
serves for unknown paths. Previously visitors got GitHub's generic page.

`noindex, follow`, in the site's own design, and bilingual: both language trees
listed side by side with a one-line description of each page, since a single
404 has to serve visitors arriving in either language. Excluded from the sitemap
automatically.

This needed a new `noindex` prop on `Layout.astro`, since the robots meta was
hardcoded to `index, follow`.

```
  Last crawl                 Sep 10, 2026, 4:45:27 PM
  User-declared canonical    https://grokbot.se/sv/
  Google-selected canonical  https://grokbot.se/
```

Google fetched the new pages, compared them against the copies it already holds
of the old URLs, found them identical, and kept the old URL as canonical. It has
not yet recrawled `/installning/` or `/` since the redirect stubs went live at
12:49 on the 10th, so as far as its index is concerned the old Swedish pages are
still sitting there being the originals.

`/sv/` being folded into `/` is the same thing one level up: Google's stored copy
of `/` is the pre-i18n Swedish homepage, so the new Swedish homepage looks like a
duplicate of it.

This is a transient state and it resolves itself when the old URLs get recrawled.
The question is whether we can hurry it, and whether the stubs are shaped right.

## Request indexing does not work on the old URLs

Tried it on `/installning/`. Rejected:

> **Indexing request rejected.** During live testing, indexing issues were
> detected with the URL.

Which is correct behaviour: Astro's generated stub carries `noindex`, and you
cannot ask Google to index a page that asks not to be indexed. So yesterday's
decision to skip these was right, though not for the reason I gave.

## The `noindex` on the stubs is the wrong signal, and it is now removed

Looking at the generated stub again:

```html
<meta http-equiv="refresh" content="0;url=/sv/installning/">
<meta name="robots" content="noindex">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://grokbot.se/sv/installning/">
```

The canonical says "consolidate this URL into that one". The `noindex` says
"drop this URL". Those are different instructions, and Google is free to act on
the second and skip the transfer. For four URLs that are the only Swedish
presence the site has in Google, and that carried 38 impressions and a click in
their last four days, losing the consolidation is the expensive outcome.

Google's documented pattern for a permanent move on a host that cannot issue a
301 is the refresh plus the canonical. Not `noindex`.

Astro's `redirects:` config always adds `noindex` and gives no way to turn it
off, so the config block is gone and the four stubs are now real pages built
from a shared `src/components/Moved.astro`:

```html
<meta http-equiv="refresh" content="0;url=/sv/installning/">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://grokbot.se/sv/installning/">
```

Since they are pages now rather than config redirects, `@astrojs/sitemap` would
have listed them, so the sitemap gained a `filter` that excludes the four.

Verified in `dist/`: 17 pages built (12 content, 4 stubs, 404), all four stubs
carry refresh and canonical and no `noindex`, sitemap still lists exactly 12 URLs
with no stubs among them.
